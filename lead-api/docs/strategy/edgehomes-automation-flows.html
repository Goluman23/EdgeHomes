01The map

Five channels · one desk

Every lead, whatever door they use, becomes one contact with a source, a score and a next step. Instagram and ads capture; WhatsApp qualifies and books; email carries long content and seasonal campaigns; people only step in where judgement or money is involved.

Capture

  • Instagram comments, DMs, stories, ads
  • Meta lead forms, click-to-WhatsApp
  • Google, website, QR at societies
  • Calls, email, referrals

Filter

  • 4 button questions
  • Lead score 0–100
  • Hot / Warm / Cold
  • Spam & job filters

Convert

  • Floor plan → 3D studio visit
  • Reminders → walk-in
  • Proposal → decision
  • Booking amount → won

Keep

  • Nurture by possession date
  • Seasonal campaigns
  • Project updates & handover
  • Reviews & referrals
The one rule: no contact is ever without a next step. Every contact is always in exactly one stage — new, qualifying, qualified, visit booked, visited, proposal, won, nurture, lost (with reason), opted out. A 9 PM check finds anything that slipped.

03Filter & score

Many chats in · few studio visits out

Four tap-to-answer questions and two signals give every lead a score from 0 to 100. The score decides who gets a designer call in 15 minutes, who books themselves, and who goes to nurture — so the team only spends time on chats that can become projects.

The 4 questions (buttons, never typing)

  1. City & society — Faridabad / Gurugram / Noida / Palwal / Other → society list
  2. Possession — Keys in hand / Within 3 months / 3–6 months / Later / Renovating my home
  3. Property — 2 BHK / 3 BHK / 4 BHK+ / Builder floor / Villa / Plot to build / Office
  4. Budget — ₹5–8 L / ₹8–15 L / ₹15–25 L / ₹25 L+ / Not sure yet

"Not sure yet" opens a mini price card by BHK, then asks again — budget is never left blank.

Before any menu, one universal listener reads every incoming message — words, emoji, a number, a photo, a voice note, a location pin — checks who the contact is and where they are in the flow, answers that, and brings them back to their next step. Location, contact, timings

Burst rule

Three messages in 10 seconds ("hi" / "price?" / "location?") are grouped and answered once, in order — never three separate menus.

Repeat rule

The same message sent twice, or "hello??" after a reply, means we missed something — a person takes over.

Long message rule

Anything over ~40 words is a brief, not a keyword: saved to the contact and handed to a designer with a summary.

Never dead-end

Every reply ends with a button, a question or a named person. No "I didn't understand" twice.

After hours

9 PM – 9 AM: answers still go out; calls and human requests get a call-back time picker and a 10 AM promise.

Build in ManyChat

Default reply → router flow; keyword rules ("contains") for each intent list; conditions on stage tags; every user-input block's "not matched" path → router. Confirm AI-intent options in your ManyChat plan.

09Running many chats at once

Inbox rules · roles · SLAs

When dozens of chats run in parallel, the risk is not the bot — it is two people answering the same lead, or nobody answering a hot one. These rules keep the shared inbox calm.

Roles

  • Inbox owner (9 AM–3 PM, 3 PM–9 PM shifts): first human replies, assigns
  • Designers: hot calls, 3D prep, proposals
  • Project manager: active clients only
  • Founder: ₹25 L+ leads, plots, decision meetings, complaints

Assignment

  • Hot → round-robin to designers, by city
  • Same lead always keeps the same designer
  • Active client → their PM
  • Unassigned > 10 min → inbox owner alert
  • One person per chat; others leave notes

Reply-time SLAs

  • Bot: instant, 24×7
  • Hot lead: call within 15 min
  • needs_human: 5 min (9 AM–9 PM)
  • Active client: 2 h
  • Email: 2 h
  • Breach → manager alert + apology message
